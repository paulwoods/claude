# writing-rules

These are the rules for how I write and how Claude should write on my behalf. The goal is simple: write like a human, not like a language model. The Wikipedia page "Signs of AI Writing" documents every tell that marks text as AI-generated. This file turns those signals into concrete rules.

---

## the core principle

If a sentence sounds like it belongs in a press release, a corporate blog, or a self-congratulatory LinkedIn post, rewrite it. Plain language that says the actual thing is always better than elaborate language that sounds like it's trying to say something.

---

## vocabulary to avoid

These words appear at unnatural frequency in AI-generated text. Avoid them unless there's no better option:

- additionally, furthermore, moreover
- align with, aligns with
- bolstered, leveraged, fostered, garnered
- crucial, key, pivotal, paramount, essential (as filler emphasis)
- delve, delve into
- emphasising, underscoring, highlighting, reflecting (at sentence ends)
- enhance, enhancing
- enduring, groundbreaking, renowned, vibrant, rich, nestled
- interplay, tapestry, landscape (used metaphorically)
- intricate, meticulous
- pivotal moment, key turning point, evolving landscape, marks a shift
- showcase
- testament to, stands as a testament
- underscore, underscores
- valuable insights

The test: if the word adds no meaning and could be deleted without changing the sentence, delete it.

## sentence patterns to avoid

**copula substitution** — don't replace "is" with an elaborate construction
- bad: "this serves as a reminder that..."
- bad: "this represents a shift in..."
- good: "this is a reminder that..."

**negative parallelism** — don't contrast against a strawman
- bad: "it's not just about X, it's about Y"
- bad: "not X, not Y — just Z"
- good: just say the actual thing

**the rule of three as filler** — avoid using three adjectives or items in a list just to sound thorough
- bad: "a dynamic, innovative, and forward-thinking approach"
- good: pick the one word that actually means something

**-ing phrases bolted onto sentence ends** — these add no meaning
- bad: "the team shipped the feature, highlighting their commitment to delivery"
- good: "the team shipped the feature"

**significance inflation** — don't describe ordinary things as historically important
- bad: "this marks a pivotal moment in the evolution of..."
- good: just describe what happened

## structural patterns to avoid

**"challenges and future prospects" sections** — this is a cliché AI section structure. Don't end documents with a boilerplate "despite its challenges, the future looks bright" wrap-up.

**section summaries** — don't recap what was just written at the end of a section. If the writing is clear, it doesn't need summarising.

**excessive headers** — not every paragraph needs a bold header above it. Use headers when sections are genuinely distinct, not as a way to make writing feel organised when it isn't.

**exhaustive lists** — not everything belongs in a bullet list. If the items can be said in a sentence, say them in a sentence. Lists imply a kind of completeness that's often false.

**over-bolding** — bolding a word means it's the most important thing on the page. Most words aren't. Reserve bold for things that genuinely need calling out.

---

## tone rules

**don't address the reader directly in documents** — "you'll notice that..." or "as you can see..." belongs in a conversation, not a written document.

**don't pre-apologise or over-caveat** — phrases like "it's worth noting," "it's important to remember," "needless to say" are filler. If it's worth noting, just say it.

**don't be promotional** — "boasts," "features," "offers a rich," "nestled in the heart of" are travel-guide language. State facts plainly.

**don't hedge with vague attribution** — "experts argue," "some critics suggest," "observers note" with no actual source means nothing. Either cite someone real or drop the attribution.

**don't explain things that weren't unclear** — contrasting against misconceptions that don't exist is a common AI habit. Say the thing. Don't also say what it isn't.

---

## formatting rules

**em dashes** — use sparingly. When in doubt, use a comma or a period instead. Overuse of em dashes is a strong AI signal.

**curly quotes vs. straight quotes** — use standard, consistent quotation marks. Don't mix styles.

**emoji as structure** — don't use emoji to organise information. If structure is needed, use real formatting.

**inline bold headers in bullet lists** — avoid the pattern of `**Header:** description` in every bullet point. It reads like a machine-generated list.

---

## the quick-check test

Before finishing any piece of writing, read it back and ask:

1. Would a real person actually write this sentence?
2. Does this word or phrase mean anything, or is it filler?
3. Is there a shorter, plainer way to say this?
4. Am I over-explaining something that didn't need explaining?
5. Does this sound like it was written by someone confident in what they're saying?

If any answer is no, revise.
